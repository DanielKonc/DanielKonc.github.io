Text 1, theme: It Ends with a Mistake

It was after the Third World War and the Final World War. The invention of cold fusion. The conquest of space. The First and Second Colonial Genocides, the discovery of extraterrestrial life. After the Black Campaign and the Endless Invasion. After all contact with anything extraterrestrial had been severed.

Dr Kelley saved us. He saved human civilisation on the verge of collapse after so many unfulfilled dreams and so many failed attempts. Homo sapiens were finally ready to give up, slip into decline and slowly disappear onto the rubbish heap of history. He gave us hope. A reason to exist. The Philosopher's Stone, the material of infinity. Like something out of fiction, except real. All we had to do was wait. To have every wish fulfilled, all we had to do was wait.

And so Concordia was born. The Empire of Dreams. People changed. They abolished money, the endless pursuit of power and wealth. Peace prevailed. Not for a year, but for thousands of years. One planet, one state, generation after generation building a better tomorrow, knowing they would never live to see it. The time estimate was clear — 11,233 years.

Concordia. An empire willing to wait for a miracle. It simply failed to realise that it already had one. At first, the entire planet stood ready, waiting for the experiment to be completed. But as time went on, more and more people grew bored and returned to their lives, leaving behind a single team of scientists to watch, day and night, for that window of just a few seconds.

And when the moment finally came, Oswald Burrows was on the toilet, Kevin McCay was at the coffee machine, and Marcin Szinkiewicz was yawning. When he finally noticed what was happening, he panicked. The neurotranslators misread his thoughts, and the machine designed to stop the transformation did the exact opposite.

And that's the end, Radim. Don't be afraid. Can you hear those explosions outside? It won't be long now.

Text 2, theme: Crack the Eggs into a Cup

[S]: "No, I'm not doing it. I'm not a child anymore."

[M]: "You never minded before. Look at this beautiful little cup. I found it the day you hatched."

[S]: "That's exactly the point. The others laugh right in my face. They say I'm a mummy's boy and let myself be fed as if I'd only just hatched."

[M]: "Forget about the others. If they want to stretch their necks like cobras just because of tradition, that's their business. But it's uncomfortable, painful and completely unnecessary, and as your mother, I forbid it."

[S]: "I'm an adult now! And I'm going to eat like one. It's not my fault you have these maternal instincts. If you need that so badly, go lay another clutch. Just leave me alone."

[M]: "I only want what's best for you, sweetheart."

[S]: "But you're only making everything worse. You embarrass me in front of my friends. You won't even let me eat like an adult. I hate you!"

The young snake, barely past infancy, turned and quickly slithered away between the clumps of grass. The adult female remained where she was for a while, swaying her head above a quail egg resting gently against the side of a small, faded ceramic cup, before slowly disappearing in the opposite direction. Only then did two men carrying cameras cautiously emerge from the bushes.

"Sometimes I'd really like to know what they say to each other. That thing with the cup wasn't a coincidence," one of them remarked, thoughtfully replaying in his mind the scene they had just witnessed.

"Stop talking and come on. This egg-eating snake documentary isn't going to film itself," the other grunted. They both hoisted their cameras and disappeared among the trees.

Text 4, theme: To Boldly Go…

…without fear or reproach, if your mind is keen and your heart is pure, if you long for adventure, beauty, joy and adventure, then join us! You will experience things never experienced before and learn to expect the unexpected. Sudden twists, danger, and the warm embrace of friends at your side as you venture where no human has gone before.

"So, what do you think? Pretty good, right?"

Jarek turned to Jonatan, watching every feature of his face with excitement in his eyes.

"I don't know. You've got 'adventure' in there twice. And don't you think you've overdone the drama a bit? We're just a hiking club, after all."

There was no hiding the doubt in his friend's voice.

Jarek merely frowned and, with a pout, began erasing the repeated word. Holding a cleaning cloth in one hand and a thick green marker in the other, he looked thoughtfully out of the window as he searched for a suitably epic replacement for "adventure."

"So what? That doesn't mean we can't experience anything interesting out there, does it?" he asked Jonatan dejectedly, still staring through the window.

"Nobody's saying that, but you make it sound like we're recruiting a crew for Voyager. That's basically lying to potential members," Jonatan replied with a shrug and turned to look through the thick pane of glass as well.

"I always liked Star Trek. Actually, we have a lot in common…" Jarek muttered, beginning to carefully write letter after letter across the canvas with his marker.

"…just like them, we're going where no human has gone before."

He looked at Jonatan, and the red rocks of Mars reflected in his eyes as the second moon majestically rose above their peaks.
