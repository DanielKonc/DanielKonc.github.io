Daniel Konc
Interactive Interview

It all starts with a theme. It can be a spark, a scene from a totally unrelated movie, a picture — really, anything. The theme is the hardest part, for it anchors the whole future story within a clearly defined framework. It's much easier afterwards. I take the idea and expand it, adding connections and building a world around it. At a certain point, I know: this is something I want to make. Usually, it's a purely emotional decision: what I've come up with is worth the effort. With shorter pieces, this is usually when I start writing, even if I don't have the final twist figured out yet. I trust the creative process to fill in the structure and content as I write, sometimes even providing the final twist. Of course, the bigger the project, the more preparation it requires, because there are more elements interacting with each other. If the story is long, I don't start writing until I've worked out all the individual parts and how they connect to the whole. And, of course, once the work is done, I go back through it and question what I've already created, add depth, and work in interesting details. Playtime. The number of iterations depends entirely on how much time I have, because I'll never be completely satisfied. There is always something that can be improved. During this process, it helps me to talk the project through with someone else, even if they don't contribute any ideas themselves. Just having someone there forces me to articulate the project, and that tends to open the floodgates for new ideas. Most of the time, though, it's somewhere in the middle. If I know I'll have room to improvise, I may have the beginning, the ending, and a few clear guideposts for how the story should develop, and leave the rest to the situation as it unfolds.
