Daniel Konc
Interactive Interview

I like detective stories that force the audience to think and put the clues together. Stories that operate in the grey area between clearly good and clearly evil, and follow the rules they set for themselves. I don't particularly like needless suffering, but when it serves a purpose, I enjoy using it — including bad endings and unmet expectations. It's quite a contrast to what comes most naturally when I write: sarcastic humour and lots of similes. I blame Jiří Kulhánek for that; his style had a huge influence on me when I was younger. Since then, I've of course learnt to write in a serious tone as well, because jokes will only get you so far, some topics demand a different approach, and I don't want to box myself in. Especially in recent years, I've been experimenting a lot with different genres and approaches. I deliberately experiment, knowing that a particular concept may not work, but that's a risk I'm willing to take. Otherwise, how would I discover all the ways it can work?
