Daniel Konc
Interactive Interview

For starters, I first encountered RPGs when I was 13, and I've been a dedicated storyteller since I was 18, especially in Vampire: The Masquerade. It suits me better than many fantasy systems, where gameplay is often centered on meeting in a tavern, slaughtering your way through an orc-infested mine, saving the princess, and planting a tree. VtM is much more about information, politics, and detective work. About the consequences of your actions and character motivations. About surviving in a world that is hostile by nature. It doesn't mean I don't like fantasy — quite the contrary — but I'm convinced that VtM gave me the ability to look at classic sword-and-sorcery settings more rationally and with greater perspective. It helps me pay attention to often-overlooked aspects of worldbuilding: the consequences of characters' actions, defences against magic if it exists and is commonly used, or the use of fantasy elements in everyday life. At the same time, I'm a dedicated text-based RPG player. I like this style because you have enough time to think about your post, and as a result, both the story and the characters become much deeper. There is time for personal motivations, relationships, reasons why the characters even stick together, and all the layers you can add one by one to flesh out the fictional world the game takes place in. The trade-off, of course, is much slower story progression.
