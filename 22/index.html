Daniel Konc
Interactive Interview

I generally focus on long, complex stories — campaign-style narratives. I love imagining worlds full of characters and relationships, and exploring what impact a small action in one place could have on events — and people — somewhere else. I love creating backgrounds, rules, and relationships.

In this respect, storytelling for a live RPG and designing a story-driven computer game are quite similar. Players act as they see fit — with freedom within set boundaries — and their decisions and actions can cover a wide range of possibilities. But while a live RPG has a storyteller who can improvise when something completely unexpected happens, a computer game is in a more difficult position. It has to define the boundaries of the player's freedom and, within those boundaries, anticipate their decisions and be prepared for them.

The worst thing that can happen is for a player to take an important but unexpected action and have neither the world nor the characters react to it because that possibility wasn't accounted for.
